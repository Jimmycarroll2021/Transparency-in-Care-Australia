# GAP-004: Services outside the NQF have no public list (473 / 142 trace)

Researched: 2026-10-29. Role: source-researcher. British English. No individuals named.

## Summary

- The 473 figure originates with the ABC Four Corners investigation itself. The ABC text says Four Corners "can reveal" it. I found no official publication (communique, estimates, Hansard, ACECQA) containing 473. [Verify This] High confidence that no official origin was located in the sources I could reach; Low confidence that none exists (the Education department and ACECQA sites were partly blocked).
- The 142 figure is attributed by the ABC to "state governments". No named official, document or date is given. No communique found. [Verify This]
- The "more than 12,000 kids" figure appears only in the programme narration and is not attributed to any dataset. The online article says "thousands of kids". [Verify This]
- There is no single national public list of non-NQF services. ACECQA's national register covers NQF services only. Public state lists exist for Queensland (confirmed, CSV on data.gov.au). For NSW, Victoria, WA and SA I found no public register (not confirmed absent). Tasmania, ACT and NT were not resolved.

## 2. Official definition of "outside the NQF"

Not verified from a primary text this session: the legislation and ACECQA guide pages returned 403 (ACECQA guide 2.1, AustLII, legislation.nsw.gov.au, ACECQA media PDFs). Wording below is from search-result summaries and regulator pages, not direct quotation of the statute. [Verify This] Quote verbatim from National Law s 5 and National Regulations reg 5 before publishing.

- Mechanism: the National Law s 5 defines "education and care service" and lists excluded service types; National Regulations reg 5 ("Services that are not education and care services") prescribes further classes. Source type official_report (legislation). Pages: https://classic.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/nsw/consol_reg/eacsnr422/s5.html (403), https://www.acecqa.gov.au/national-quality-framework/guide-nqf/section-2-applications-and-approvals/2-service-approval/21-what-education-and-care-service (403).
- Classes reported in search summaries of reg 5: occasional care (ad hoc or casual, not usually full-time or all-day, most children preschool age or under), care in a child's own home (in-home care, other than approved family day care), school holiday care for not more than 28 days in a calendar year (Queensland text), hotel or resort guest care, mobile services, care provided mainly by parents or family, and a licensed ACT playschool under the Children and Young People Act 2008 (ACT explanatory statement, https://www.legislation.act.gov.au/Static/Notes/education_national_regulations_es.pdf). [Verify This] exact paragraph wording.
- State layer: preschools/kindergartens in Tasmania, and most in Western Australia, are outside scope (ACECQA reporting, per search summary; not read directly).
- Practical definition used in the Four Corners context: services regulated under state law or funding rules rather than the National Law, including CCCFR services under the Child Care Subsidy Minister's Rules 2017.

## 5. Safeguards that differ

- Official source reached: South Australian Education Standards Board residual services page (https://esb.sa.gov.au/early-childhood/residual-services, retrieved 2026-10-29, official_report). A modified National Law applies. Chapter 4 operational requirements are modified; educator-to-child ratios and qualifications for occasional care, rural and mobile care, and Schedule 1 services are set by Board determinations (Instruments A, B, C) that vary by class. The Board "acknowledges some requirements are hard for these services to meet" (paraphrase of page; verify wording). The page does not say whether residual services receive assessment and ratings.
- Queensland: the Education and Care Services Act 2013 "supports quality outcomes and adopts elements of the National Law for some small-scale services" (https://earlychildhood.qld.gov.au/regulation/education-and-care-services-act). That page does not address ratios, qualifications, ratings, sleep or transport. No official statement was found on sleep supervision, transport checks or quality rating for non-NQF services. [Verify This]
- NSW: the Supplementary Provisions Act was updated in 2018 to align standards with the National Law (https://education.nsw.gov.au/early-childhood-education/regulation-and-compliance/regulation-assessment-and-rating/regulatory-framework). Whether NSW non-NQF services are assessed and rated was not stated.
- Four Corners claim (media): the 473 services operate "without the same national standards and scrutiny". Not independently verified. Absence of a flag is not evidence of safety.
